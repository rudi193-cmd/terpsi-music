## 2. Top finds, with the concrete transplant and its cost

### 2.1 Cal-ITP Benefits — invisible aid, implemented, in government production

This is the single best find in the slice and I did not expect it to exist. It is California's transit-discount enrolment system, and its architecture is a working answer to §7's *"waivers and scholarships applied so that no student, and no other family, can tell who is on assistance."*

Three verified properties. First, the app's own documentation states *"The application requires no user accounts and stores no information about the user,"* with identity, Medicare and card details handled by third parties and therefore *"inaccessible to the team."* Second, eligibility travels as *"signed and encrypted JSON Web Tokens containing only the most necessary of user data"* — the wire format is JWS-inside-JWE via `jwcrypto`, verified in `eligibility_api/tokens.py` and `eligibility_api/server.py`. Third, and this is the mechanic worth the whole report: enrolment does not produce a list of discounted riders that anyone at the point of service reads. It adds the rider's tokenized funding source to a **concession group** at the payment processor — `_get_group_funding_source(client, group_id, funding_source_id)` in `benefits/enrollment_littlepay/enrollment.py`, with an expiry on the membership. The discount is computed on the payment rail. The driver, the fare inspector and the other riders in the queue observe an ordinary tap.

**Transplant.** A fee waiver must never be a field on the student, a flag on the roster, or a reduced number on a line item a parent volunteer can see. It is a **membership in a fee group that the charge-generation step consults, and nothing else reads.** Concretely: `FeeSchedule` resolves an amount for a `(student, activity)` pair by group membership; `Charge` is written already-correct; there is no "waiver" column anywhere on `Charge`, and no view or export can join a student to their group. The `invalid_at` discipline (rule 3) gives you Cal-ITP's expiry for free. This also composes with §7's refusal-indistinguishability test: the assertion to write is that a student in the reduced group and a student who simply owes less produce identical rows, identical counts and identical subject lists at every surface.

**Cost.** AGPL-3.0, Python/Django — vendoring the app is a licence event, so take the mechanic and write it. The uncomfortable part is that Cal-ITP achieves invisibility by pushing the discount to a **third-party processor**, which is exactly the move rule 1 and §6 exist to prevent. Our version must put the group on the local hub and keep it behind the egress gate — which means we get the dignity property but must build the group-resolution ourselves rather than renting it. Estimate: small (a table, a resolver, and a predicate), and the tests are the real work.

### 2.2 Apache Fineract's maker-checker — the treasurer-turnover answer, verified in source

§7 asks for an *"audit trail sufficient to survive a booster treasurer turnover."* Fineract has the enforcement, not a ledger, and I read it rather than trusting the README.

`Permission.java` (`fineract-core/.../useradministration/domain/Permission.java`) carries a column `can_maker_checker`, so four-eye is a **property of an individual permission**, not a global mode. `CommandSourceService.java` checks `configurationDomainService.isMakerCheckerEnabledForTask(permission)` and, if the acting user is not the checker, parks the command instead of applying it; on approval it calls `commandSource.markAsChecked(user)`. There is a dedicated API — `MakercheckersApiResource.java`, tagged *"Maker Checker (or 4-eye) functionality"* — for listing, approving and deleting parked entries, and `AuditsApiResource.java` states *"Every non-read API request is audited. A fully processed request can not be changed or deleted."* `PermissionCantBeMakerCheckerableException` exists because some permissions must not be four-eyeable, which is a distinction our design will need too.

**Transplant.** Fineract's design is precisely rule 18's distinction done right: the queue is the enforcement, and the audit table is the ledger, and they are separate objects. For us: a `Charge` write-off, a refund, a waiver grant, and a vendor payment are maker-checker permissions; a read of a balance is not. The parked-command row is the treasurer handoff packet's raw material — every unapproved and every approved action, with who proposed and who sealed, is already the document a successor needs. Combine with rule 10 (`draft` until a named human seals it) and rule 16 (no role can delete the record of its own exercise) and the shapes line up almost exactly.

**Two things to copy carefully.** `PortfolioCommandSourceWritePlatformServiceImpl.java` contains a deliberate bypass — self-service updates skip maker-checker because *"maker checker doesnt mean anything here"* — plus a `user.isCheckerSuperUser()` escape hatch. A checker-superuser is a single volunteer who can approve their own withdrawals, which is the embezzlement path this whole feature exists to close. Copy the mechanism; **forbid the superuser**, and write the adversarial test (rule 19) that tries to have one principal both propose and approve a write-off and asserts refusal.

**Cost.** Do not run Fineract. It is a Java core-banking platform with 2,630 forks and a schema for loan products; the useful surface is roughly three tables and one interceptor at the write path. Small to build, and it must be built at the write path, because a queue that a fourth code path can route around is a ledger.

### 2.3 hledger (or Beancount) — the ledger a person can read without the app

Rule 11 says the canonical store is read-only to the app and promotion is a human act; §7 wants three-way reconciliation across district, activity and booster accounts. Plain-text accounting is the family that fits, and hledger is the one I would run: GPL-3.0-or-later, Haskell, 4,599★, pushed two days ago, and the journal is a human-readable text file so version control and diff review come free.

The load-bearing feature is **balance assertions**, which I verified in `hledger-lib/Hledger/Data/Balancing.hs` (`balanceTransactionAndCheckAssertionsB`, *"Update the account's running balance and check the balance assertion if any"*) and in the operator table in `hledger-lib/Hledger.hs` (`=`, `==`, `=*`, `==*`, plus balance *assignments*). An assertion is a claim, written in the journal at a date, that this account's balance is exactly this — and the tool refuses to parse the file if reality disagrees. That is three-way reconciliation expressed as a parse error: assert the district figure, the activity-account figure and the booster figure at each statement date, and a drift becomes a build failure rather than a report nobody reads.

**Transplant.** Agents write sidecar journal fragments; a human `include`s them into the canonical journal and adds the assertion; the app never writes the canonical file. `hledger` outputs CSV/HTML/JSON/SQL, so the app's read views are projections, and the treasurer handoff packet is a git bundle plus a PDF the successor can verify with a tool they can install in a minute. Signed git commits over the journal give tamper-evidence without immudb's licence problem (§2.6).

**Cost.** Haskell binary on the hub — a static build, not a build toolchain, so acceptable. The real cost is that hledger has no notion of authorization, so per-student lane isolation (rule 8) cannot live in the journal; the journal is the **institutional** book, and the per-student ledger stays in the lane-aware store with the journal as its reconciled aggregate. If Python matters more than robustness, Beancount (GPL-2.0, 5.8k★) plus Fava (MIT) is the swap; note Fava is an editing UI, so "read-only over the journal" is a configuration claim I did **not** verify.

### 2.4 GlobaLeaks — the anonymous hardship queue already exists, and it is whistleblower software

§7 wants an *"anonymous hardship application with a director-only queue"* and §18 wants *"anonymous need identification, so a student never has to ask publicly."* That is structurally a whistleblower intake, and there is fifteen years of hardened AGPL software for it.

Verified in source: `backend/globaleaks/handlers/auth/__init__.py` defines `login_whistleblower(session, tid, receipt, client_using_tor, ...)` — the submitter authenticates with **a receipt and nothing else**, no account, no email, and the code even checks for legacy receipt hashes shorter than 64 characters to force an upgrade. `receipt_salt` is node configuration; `db_set_receipt_hash` is called from `handlers/whistleblower/submission.py`; `change_receipt` lets a submitter rotate their own credential; `tor_onion_key` is first-class configuration. The submission object is an `InternalTip` visible only to designated recipients.

**Transplant.** A family applies for a waiver from any device, gets a code, and can come back to see the disposition without ever creating an identity in the system — which is also how rule 15 (*every ask gets a dated disposition*) gets satisfied without the ask itself becoming a record about the student. The director-only queue is the recipient set. The pairing with §7.4 I-6 is natural: the declared timebound lives on the tip, and the wait is recorded.

**Cost.** AGPLv3 with §7 additional terms — running it as a separate service alongside the app is fine; linking it in is not. There is a genuine tension to resolve, not paper over: a truly anonymous application cannot be joined to a fee group without re-identifying the family at grant time. The honest design is a two-step where the *application* is anonymous and the *grant* lands as a group membership the director never has to explain, with the linkage existing exactly once, in one audited act, and never queryable afterwards. That is a real design decision, and it belongs on §18's list.

### 2.5 Rock RMS's Anonymous Giver — collapse the attribution, keep the money

Rock RMS is church management software and I cannot use a line of it: `Dev Tools/Packaging/LICENSE.md` is the **Rock Community License**, which permits use *"SOLELY FOR INTERNAL USE BY FAITH BASED ORGANIZATIONS"* and directs everyone else to buy a commercial licence. A public-school music program is not a faith-based organization. Verdict is read-only, and the mechanic is still worth more than most things I can use.

Rock maintains a reserved system Person called the **Anonymous Giver**. `Rock/Model/CRM/Person/PersonService.cs` documents `ExpungePerson` as *"programmatically merging a given person into the Anonymous Giver record"*; `Rock/Jobs/RockCleanup.cs` runs a recurring task `"anonymous giver login"` → `RemoveAnonymousGiverUserLogins()`, i.e. the system actively re-scrubs credentials that would re-personalize the anonymous account; `RockWeb/Blocks/Crm/PersonMerge.ascx.cs` guards the reserved accounts against accidental merge; and `Rock.Blocks/Reporting/Insights.cs` filters analytics to *"persons who are alive and not an anonymous giver,"* so the anonymous principal is excluded from the reports where it would distort or expose.

**Transplant.** This is the answer to "where does a waiver's counterparty go in a double-entry book?" The books must balance, so the offsetting credit needs a payer — and the payer must not be the family. Post it against a system principal (an aid fund) whose entries are deliberately unattributable, guard that principal against merge, exclude it from per-student analytics, and re-run the scrub as a job rather than trusting a one-time migration. Rock's third detail is the one people forget: **the anonymization needs a recurring janitor**, because every new feature is a chance to re-attach an identity.

**Cost.** Zero code reuse. Half a day of design, and one adversarial test per property.

### 2.6 Payments: the honest map, including what sovereignty actually costs

§8 requires that card data never enter the system, hosted fields and tokens only, SAQ-A. Ranked by how much third party each option imports:

- **`moov-io/ach` (Apache-2.0, 555★, in production).** Generate NACHA files yourself and *"upload to your ODFI."* No card, no processor, no vault, no PCI scope at all — the bank is the only counterparty and the school already has one. The trade is operational: bank onboarding, return codes (R01/R02…) you must handle, no instant authorization, and the file itself contains routing and account numbers, which is `FINANCIAL` under our own classification and therefore an L-ladder question the moment it lands on disk. **This is the recommendation for recurring installment plans**, which is exactly where card fees hurt most anyway.
- **Hosted fields / hosted checkout at a card processor.** Necessarily a third party; the token comes back and the PAN never touches us. I could not fetch `stripe.com` to verify SAQ-A eligibility conditions, so **that specific claim is unverified in this session** — do not put "SAQ-A" in a document until someone reads the current SAQ-A wording. Note the honest cost: the processor learns which families pay, when, and how much, and a family on a waiver pays a *different amount*, so the processor is a place the aid becomes visible. Cal-ITP has that problem too; it is inherent, and it is an argument for the ACH path plus cash/check for aided families.
- **`juspay/hyperswitch` (Apache-2.0, 43.4k★)** is a trap for us. It is genuinely self-hostable, but its `hyperswitch-card-vault` is *"PCI-compliant card storage"* you would be operating. Self-hosting a vault does not reduce PCI scope, it acquires it — the opposite of §8's intent. Read-only interest.
- **`killbill` (Apache-2.0, 5.6k★)** is the right shape for §7's payment plans and installments: subscription, invoice, entitlement and payment modules with gateway plugins. Its PCI/tokenization posture is documented on a host I could not reach, so verify before adopting.
- **`btcpayserver` (MIT, 7.7k★)** is the only option with *no intermediary at all* — self-hosted, non-custodial, no card data by construction. It is also Bitcoin-only with no fiat bridge, which makes it unusable for a school collecting a $340 season fee from 150 families. Worth naming precisely because it shows the price of absolute payment sovereignty: this fleet's posture can be fully satisfied only by an instrument the users cannot use. Cash, checks and bank debit are the real sovereign rails.

### 2.7 Aggregates are where the waiver leaks — ARX, PJC, and the openSIS anti-pattern

§9 says gate the export and narrate the read. The finance case sharpens it: the export that leaks is not the roster, it is the **summary**. "Total fees collected, by section" over a drumline of eleven students, published beside a total-owed figure, identifies the waiver.

`arx-deidentifier/arx` (Apache-2.0, 732★, 4,306 commits) is the tool for this and it is the *risk analysis*, not the anonymization, that we want: it will tell you, for a given report shape and generalization, what the re-identification risk is. Run it against each proposed financial export as a test fixture. `google/private-join-and-compute` (Apache-2.0, 852★) is the sharper instrument for the specific case of a booster treasurer who needs *total aid dollars* without learning recipients — two parties compute intersection size and intersection sum only. Its README also states the honest limitation, which matters more than the capability: it assumes honest-but-curious parties, and *"the intersection-sum itself may inadvertently reveal participant identities if values are highly distinctive or the intersection is unusually small."* A program with two aided students is exactly that case. `divviup/janus` (MPL-2.0) solves it properly and requires two non-colluding aggregators, which a single district hub does not have — so the realistic answer is suppression thresholds enforced at the export gate, with ARX as the test that they are sufficient.

The anti-pattern to write tests against is in `OS4ED/openSIS-Classic` (330★, pushed today), whose `functions/WidgetsFnc.php` builds a roster search filtered on *"Student Billing Balance between X and Y"* by summing `BILLING_FEES` + `LUNCH_TRANSACTIONS` − `BILLING_PAYMENTS`. That single widget is the whole failure mode: a staff-facing student search whose filter is money. Combine it with a waiver implemented as a reduced fee amount and any volunteer with roster access can enumerate the aided families in one query. **Build that query as a failing test** (rule 19): assert that no roster or search surface accepts a monetary predicate, and that a fee-group member is indistinguishable from a student who owes less.

### 2.8 Continuous audit for books a volunteer keeps

`koenderks/jfa` (GPL-3.0, CRAN, 1,485 commits) is the credible piece: sample planning and evaluation against auditing standards, classical and Bayesian, plus Benford digit tests, and it powers JASP's Audit module so there is a GUI a non-programmer treasurer or an auditing parent can drive. `milcent/benford_py` (BSD-3-Clause, 155★) is the Python-native equivalent aimed explicitly at accounting and audit. `carloscinelli/benford.analysis` (R, 65★) is the classic forensic-analytics toolkit.

**Transplant, with a caution.** Benford on a booster's books is mostly theatre and should be labelled as such. Season fees are a fixed schedule, so first digits are not Benford-distributed by construction, and a conformity test on 400 identical $340 charges tells you nothing. Where digit tests earn their place is the **unstructured** tail: reimbursements, concession deposits, and vendor invoices — free-form amounts entered by many hands, which is also where small-org theft lives. Run `jfa`'s sampling for the annual review and the handoff packet, and treat any anomaly output as §19's rule: *a human being to check on, not a metric* — and never as a finding about a named volunteer, which would be a standing cross-context score (rule 4).

## 3. Invisible-aid mechanics — the dedicated section

The project already states the general principle in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §7: *"Refusal is indistinguishable from absence… the system must not leak the fact of a refusal,"* explicitly noting this is §18's fee-waiver requirement generalized. What the scouting adds is a set of **mechanisms** that have been built by other people, and a set of leaks they each close.

**M1 — The discount lives on the rail, not on the record.** *(Cal-ITP, verified: `GroupFundingSource` with expiry.)* Aid is membership in a fee group consulted only by charge generation. There is no waiver field, no discount line, no "reduced" status. A charge is born correct. Corollary: **there must be no place where the pre-waiver amount and the post-waiver amount both appear**, because the difference is the disclosure. This is the strongest single mechanic in the report.

**M2 — Collapse the counterparty, not the transaction.** *(Rock RMS, verified: `ExpungePerson` merging into Anonymous Giver, plus a recurring login scrub and exclusion from analytics.)* Double entry needs a payer for the offsetting credit. Use a system aid-fund principal that is guarded against merge, excluded from per-student reporting, and **re-scrubbed by a recurring job** rather than a one-time migration. The money is fully auditable in aggregate; the attribution is deliberately destroyed at the point where it would name a family.

**M3 — The application never creates an identity.** *(GlobaLeaks, verified: `login_whistleblower(receipt, …)`, salted `receipt_hash`, `change_receipt`, Tor onion config, recipient-restricted tips.)* A family asks with no account and returns with a code. This is also how rule 15's dated disposition is delivered without the ask becoming a durable record *about* the student. The open design question, stated plainly: an anonymous application and a group membership must be linked exactly once, by one named human, in one audited act, and never be jointly queryable afterwards.

**M4 — Eliminate the determination rather than hide it.** The strongest form of invisible aid is not concealment but **universality**: if the fee is the same for everyone because the program funds the difference centrally, there is no status to conceal and no application to file. I believe this is the design of the USDA's Community Eligibility Provision for school meals, and I want to be exact about my epistemic state: **I could not fetch `fns.usda.gov` or `ecfr.gov` in this session, so that is recall, not a verified source.** It is also the highest-leverage item in the whole slice, because it dissolves M1–M3 rather than implementing them. §6 lists it as an open research task, and it should be resolved before the waiver schema is written, since "universal fee, centrally subsidized" and "per-student waiver group" are different tables.

**M5 — Decide by rule, not by ranking.** *(OpenFisca, AGPL-3.0.)* Rule 6 forbids computing a priority between two students, and §18 wants *"instrument lending prioritized by need rather than by who asked first."* A rules-as-code sliding scale — income band in, fee band out, tested with declarative fixtures — produces an *eligibility*, not a *ranking*, and therefore does not compare two children. Where genuine scarcity remains (one tuba, three students), rule 6 stands: the system presents, a human decides, and the correct software contribution is a **lottery with a recorded seed**, not a score. I found no open-source implementation of need-based allocation that avoids ranking; that gap is real.

**M6 — Aggregates need a suppression floor, tested.** *(ARX for risk measurement; PJC's own warning that small intersections leak; openSIS's balance-range roster search as the concrete anti-pattern.)* Every financial export and every count is a surface that inherits the indistinguishability obligation. And note §7's own caveat about the existing test: indistinguishability passes vacuously if the predicate returns nothing to anyone, so each aid-related fixture needs a companion principal who can genuinely see something.

**M7 — Tamper-evidence without a licence problem.** Do not reach for immudb: it is **BSL 1.1**, source-available and not open source, which is a poor fit for a school hub and worse for a template other programs are meant to instantiate. A plain-text journal in git with signed commits gives append-only history, third-party verifiability, and a format a successor can read with no software of ours — and it is the same artifact as the handoff packet. `hoophq/sequence` (Apache-2.0) demonstrates the hash-chain-plus-Merkle design but binds to DynamoDB, so it is a reference, not a dependency.

**M8 — What none of these solve.** The processor still sees that one family pays less. The bank statement still shows it. A parent volunteer counting concession cash still knows who did not pay for a meal at the long rehearsal (§18). Software-side invisibility has a boundary at the point where money meets people, and the design should say so out loud rather than imply a guarantee it cannot keep.

## 4. Weirdest things I found

1. **The anonymous-hardship queue is whistleblower software, and the best implementation is `globaleaks`.** A family asking for help and a source leaking documents need the same primitive: submit without identity, return with a receipt, be visible only to a named small set of recipients. GlobaLeaks has salted receipt hashes, receipt rotation, Tor onion configuration, and per-tip encryption, all verified in `backend/globaleaks/handlers/`. I would never have searched for "whistleblowing" while designing a fee-waiver form.

2. **A transit-fare discount system is the state of the art in dignified aid.** `cal-itp/benefits` — California DOT, AGPL-3.0, in production since 2022 — puts the discount on the payment rail via a processor concession **group** so nobody at the point of service can tell. It even documents that the app *"requires no user accounts and stores no information about the user."* A school fee waiver and a reduced transit fare are the same problem, and the transit people solved it first and published the code.

3. **Church software has a first-class `Anonymous Giver` person, complete with a janitor job.** Rock RMS supports merging a real human's contributions into a reserved system principal (`ExpungePerson`), guards that principal against accidental merge, excludes it from analytics, and runs `RemoveAnonymousGiverUserLogins()` on a schedule to keep it from being re-personalized. Millennia of practice around not letting the congregation see who gave what, encoded as a nightly cleanup task. And it is behind a licence that permits only faith-based organizations to use it, which is its own small irony.

4. **Fineract's four-eye feature ships with a documented way to defeat it.** The same codebase that implements per-permission maker-checker also contains `user.isCheckerSuperUser()` and a comment that for self-service updates *"maker checker doesnt mean anything here."* The best open-source implementation of segregation of duties I found also demonstrates the exact hole through which a single volunteer treasurer would walk. It is the clearest available argument for rule 19: a guard that cannot be shown to fail has not been shown to work.

5. **Three-way reconciliation is most robustly expressed as a parse error.** hledger's balance assertions (`hledger-lib/Hledger/Data/Balancing.hs`) mean the journal simply will not load if the asserted district / activity / booster balance disagrees with the postings. Not a report, not a dashboard flag, not an email nobody opens — the file is invalid until a human reconciles it. That is the strictest reconciliation mechanism in the report and it comes from a 4,600-star command-line tool aimed at people tracking their groceries.

6. **Honourable mention: the only truly sovereign payment processor is unusable.** `btcpayserver` accepts money with no intermediary whatsoever and cannot accept a check from a parent. It is the empirical price tag on absolute payment sovereignty, and the reason cash and bank debit belong in the design.

## 5. Gaps — what does not exist, stated as absence rather than result

- **There is no open-source booster/PTA treasurer application.** GitHub repo search for booster-club treasurer software returned zero results; a search for PTA treasurer returned two personal repos with zero stars (`deepssharma/pta_treasurer`, `marneborn/pta-treasurer`, both effectively empty). The segment is served entirely by subscription SaaS. The prior art for this requirement is therefore **documentary** (association money-handling procedures, state student-activity-fund accounting manuals) and I could not fetch any of it — see §6.
- **No open-source implementation of need-based allocation that avoids ranking people.** Nothing found for prioritizing instrument lending by need without producing a score. Rule 6 territory; likely has to be written, and a recorded-seed lottery is the honest primitive.
- **Mutual aid, timebanking and community-currency software is thin and mostly abandoned.** `topic:mutual-aid` (56 repos) and `topic:complementary-currency` (4 repos) are dominated by prototypes and manifestos; the only production-looking timebank is `nexus-v1` (AGPL-3.0, 2★). ROSCA/savings-group ledger search returned nothing usable. I expected more here and found less; the honest verdict is that this literature is a source of *ideas*, not of code. Apache Fineract is the one serious piece of group-savings engineering in the space.
- **No open-source encoding of the IRS individual-fundraising-account constraint was located.** Open Collective's fiscal-host model is the most plausible prior art — money held by the host, disbursed against expenses, no personal balances — but the ownership semantics live in documentation I could not reach, so this is **unverified**. Meanwhile `FundraisingCredit` is already in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §8's money model, and a per-student fundraising credit is exactly the shape the constraint is about. This is the one place where a schema decision is pending on unresearched law.

## 6. Open research tasks handed back (blocked by egress, not by effort)

1. **The IRS constraint on individual fundraising accounts.** Needs the actual authority read before `FundraisingCredit` gets a schema. Blocked hosts: `irs.gov`, court reporters. Highest priority of the three — it is the only item here where getting it wrong is a legal exposure rather than a design defect.
2. **Whether universal-fee funding is available, and how school-meal programs did it (M4).** Needs `fns.usda.gov` and `ecfr.gov`, including the overt-identification prohibition and the Community Eligibility Provision. Resolving this may make the entire waiver schema unnecessary, so it should precede it.
3. **Current SAQ-A eligibility wording.** Needs `stripe.com` / PCI SSC. `ARCHITECTURE.md` §8 already asserts SAQ-A; nobody in this session read the standard, and the assertion should carry that provenance until somebody does.
