v4. Earlier drafts are in git history and are not the version. v2 fixed
structure — the father moved up so the bridge could land on him, the pre
turned instead of restating, the last chorus varied. v3 fixed mechanics:
verse 3 scanned 6/5/5/5 against 6/6/6/6 everywhere else, only verse 1
rhymed, the point of view wobbled, and one flat abstraction sat in a verse's
weakest position. Four of those five were found by counting rather than by
ear, and the fifth by the checker.

v4 is by ear alone, because after v3 the checker had nothing left to say.
Three lines:

    - "Hands cold, don't wait" → "Hands cold, horns up". The old third line
      was filler that rhymed, which is the worst kind: it existed because
      -ait was easy, and it told the listener nothing they did not already
      know. "Horns up" is what a drum major actually says, it is the physical
      instant before sound, and it deliberately breaks the chorus's AAAA so
      the one non-hook line stops sounding like the hook.
    - Same in the out-chorus, keeping "cold" → "warm" as the only word that
      changes across the song.
    - "He couldn't have called you from there" → "And he couldn't call you
      from there". Same syllable count, one fewer conditional. A lyric has no
      room for the past perfect.

    None of this was checkable, which is the point. The checker found five
    defects in v2 and v3 and proposed no replacement for any of them; it had
    nothing at all to say about v4.

ARRANGEMENT

The one idea everything follows from: it is a song about a marching band
that must not sound like one until the end. The band is the payoff, not the
texture. Horns in verse one spend the only card the song has.

So the arrangement assembles the ensemble in real time, the way people
arrive in a lot.

    F minor. 76-84 BPM, half-time feel, so the count of eight sits heavy.
    Field tempo is 120-144 and going there would make it a march, which
    this is not. The tag implies double time and never arrives.

VERSE 1     One voice. Solo trumpet on long tones underneath. No drums --
            nothing where the beat should be, so the listener does not yet
            know the tempo. Cold brass runs flat and the pitch wavers; that
            instability is the verse and cannot be faked.

VERSE 2     The pulse arrives as hazard lights. Bass drum, mallet, soft,
            slow and even. First grid in the song. Still no snare.

PRE         A second voice, a third above. The only harmony so far, and it
            lands on the turn. Voices entering as people arriving.

CHORUS 1    Marching snare and voice. Street beat, bass drum on the
            downbeat, and still no horns -- held back one section longer
            than anyone expects.

VERSE 3     Low reeds, clarinets at the bottom of the range, woody and
            cold. Snare drops to a whisper.

CHORUS 2    Brass enters wrong on purpose: unison, low, no harmony, no
            brightness. The section without the chord. Power, no colour.

BRIDGE      Strip to voice and the verse-one trumpet, back on the same long
            tone -- steady now, because the lips are warm. Nobody notices
            that consciously and everybody feels it.

            On "your father's still out there parked," lift to Ab major.
            Warmth arrives harmonically and texturally on the same word.

CHORUS OUT  Full ensemble, and the singer does not push. The vocal stays
            exactly where it was and the band comes up around it. The kid
            does not get louder; the world does.

TAG         Everything cuts. Voice alone: "Eleven minutes / Eleven minutes."
            On "Go" the whole band hits once, together, and stops dead.
            Silence. End.

            The alternative -- "Go" launching twenty seconds of drum break
            -- is what everyone would do, and it turns the song into the
            show it is about. One layer too literal.

NOT IN IT   Drum kit, guitars, comping piano. With a kit it is a pop song
            about a band; with a battery it is the band.

VOICE       Young and untrained. Drifting slightly off the grid in the cold
            verses, locked by the last chorus -- the rhythm tightening as
            the body warms up.
