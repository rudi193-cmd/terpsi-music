v2. The checker was run after the draft was finished and not during it,
which is the order §24 argues for — a checker consulted mid-line writes the
line. It reported three findings, and fixing one introduced a fourth.

Two of those four were real:

    - VERSE 3 opened on "rise" and closed on "tight", and "rise"/"white"
      is assonance, so the verse read AABA where its siblings read ABCB.
      Audible once pointed at and not before. → "Now the buses top the
      grade".
    - "Hat pulled tight" was three syllables against four in the last line
      of both other verses. → "Hat pulled down tight".

**Two were the checker being wrong**, and both of them turned on "grade":
`rhyme_key` dropped a silent final e without recording that it had, so
every vowel-consonant-e word was filed under its *short* vowel. "grade"
sat in the class of "glad", which made it rhyme with "glass" and with
"all". Two lines here were revised to break collisions that do not exist.

That is the loosest error the tool can make — it does not miss a scheme,
it invents one, and a writer regularising a verse against an invented
scheme rewrites lines that were already right. Fixed in `craft/text.py`,
with the guard at `tests/test_craft.py` and the mutation row in
`tests/ablate.py`; "time"/"him" and "hate"/"hat" were perfect rhymes
before it and are not now.

Both lines stayed changed anyway, which is a judgement and not a result:

    - "Plume bent flat on the glass" → "flat to the roof". A shako on a
      school bus does bend against the ceiling. A window does not require
      the plume to be bent at all, so the original image was decorative
      and did not know it.
    - "Low brass coming up the grade" → "and a mile of road". The verse
      now says "top the grade" and the pre no longer needs to; and "a long
      way", which this replaced, was the only abstraction in the song.

The checker has nothing left to say about this draft. That is not the same
as the draft being finished, and after this session it is specifically not
the same, because for two of four findings it was the thing that was wrong.

ARRANGEMENT

The one idea everything follows from, and it is the companion piece's idea
inverted: `get-ready.txt` withholds the band until the end and then lets it
arrive. Here the band never arrives. It is heard at a distance for the whole
song, it crosses the frame twice, and it does not get closer either time.
The man singing is a mile of road away from it and stays there.

    D major, 88 BPM, straight. Not a minor key and not slow: he is not
    asking to be pitied and the arrangement must not do it for him. The
    only sad thing in the song is where he is standing.

    The band is a real ensemble recorded outdoors at two hundred yards,
    off-axis, on one microphone. It must not be a sample played quietly.
    Distance takes the high end off brass and leaves the bass drum, and no
    reverb added to a close recording does that. It should also be very
    slightly sharp against the track and never corrected -- a cold band
    warming up is not at A440 and the mismatch is the fence.

VERSE 1     Acoustic guitar, thumb and two fingers. Upright bass on one
            and three. No drums at all, ever -- see NOT IN IT.

VERSE 2     Nothing added. The admission is the plainest thing in the
            song and gets the plainest setting in it. Resisting an
            instrument here is the arrangement.

PRE         The distant band enters underneath, low brass only, and the
            listener should not be certain it is there. First pass.

CHORUS      Pedal steel, one held note under the whole thing, no fills.
            Guitar stays where it was. Voice does not lift.

VERSE 3     The band is closest here and still far. Bass drum audible for
            the first time. It passes across the stereo field left to
            right over the four lines and is gone by the last one, which
            is what a bus does.

BRIDGE      Guitar out. Bass and voice. On "I come off this road in
            November" the pedal steel moves for the first time in the
            song, one step up, and holds again.

CHORUS OUT  Everything that has played comes back. Nothing new arrives,
            because nothing new has happened to him -- one word changed
            in the last line and that is the whole event.

TAG         Voice and nothing. On "Go on" the singer stops.

            The band does not. It carries on at its distance for another
            fifteen seconds and fades out mid-figure, unresolved, still a
            mile off. `get-ready.txt` ends with the whole band hitting
            once together and stopping dead; this one is the same moment
            from the shoulder of the road, where it does not stop, it
            just gets further away.

NOT IN IT   Any drum in the room. A kit dates it, and a marching battery
            close-miked would put him at the show. He is not at the show.
            No fiddle -- it would make the sound of the man's feelings,
            and the point of the song is that he does not express them.

VOICE       A man in his forties who has told this story before and is
            not enjoying telling it again. No vibrato. Slightly behind
            the beat and never dragging: the difference is whether he
            sounds tired or sounds reluctant, and he sounds reluctant.
